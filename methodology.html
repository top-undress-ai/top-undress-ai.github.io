How We Tested 7 Undress AI Apps in 2026

Published August 1, 2026 · 14-day benchmark · 2,800 data points · Reproducible

This page documents the full methodology behind our 2026 undress AI mobile benchmark. If you want to see the results, go to the full reviews or the summary rankings. If you want to verify the numbers yourself, the raw CSV lives on GitLab.

Why We Ran This Test

Most "best undress AI" lists in 2025-2026 are ranked by affiliate payout, not by user experience. We wanted to know which apps actually work well on a phone — the device 89% of these users are on, according to referrer data from public forums. So we ignored desktop benchmarks and tested every app the way a real user would use it: on a phone, on real networks, with the free tier first.

Test Devices

iPhone 15 Pro · iOS 18.5 · 8 GB RAM · A17 Pro · Safari 18 and installed PWAs

Google Pixel 8 · Android 15 · 8 GB RAM · Tensor G3 · Chrome 128 and installed PWAs

Both devices were factory-reset before the benchmark started. No VPN, no ad-blocker, no cookie-manager extensions — we wanted to measure the default experience, not an optimised one.

Volume

7 apps × 2 devices × 4 network profiles × 5 gens × 10 sessions across 14 days = 2,800 data points per metric.

Metrics We Recorded

Tap-to-result latency

Time from the moment the "generate" button is tapped to the moment the result image is fully rendered on screen. Measured with iOS screen recording at 60 fps and Android's built-in screen recorder, then counted frame by frame. This is the number users actually feel.

Data per generation

Measured via Charles Proxy on Wi-Fi runs. Includes upload of source image plus download of result. Doesn't include app shell / CSS / JS assets — only per-generation traffic.

Battery drain per 10 sequential generations

Both devices started each session at exactly 80% charge, screen at 50% brightness, all background apps closed. Ten generations were run back-to-back and the battery percentage after was recorded. Repeated 3 times per app per device, values averaged.

One-handed UX score (0-10)

Subjective, but scored against a fixed rubric: can you reach the upload button with your thumb, is the CTA anchored to the bottom, does the result screen require zoom, does the app work in portrait only, is there a floating action button obscuring content. Same rubric applied by two testers independently, scores averaged.

Push notification support

Binary: does the app send a native push when the result is ready if you background it? Tested by starting a generation, immediately switching to another app, waiting.

Install friction

Number of taps from landing page to first working generation. Signup screens, email verification, and permission dialogs all count as taps.

Free Tier vs Paid

Every app was first tested on its free tier without any promo code. Then we re-ran the benchmark with our two verified promo codes — TOP50AI (50 free generations) and UNDRESS25 (25 bonus credits, stackable). Verified redemption URL: aireview.fun/paras.

Data Retention Check

For every app we uploaded a unique test image with an embedded steganographic marker, then waited 7 days and used the app's own "delete my data" flow where available. We then checked whether the marker still returned in any reverse-image search or in the app's own gallery. Results are documented per app in the reviews.

Reproducibility

Full raw CSV (2,800 rows, one per data point), Charles session files, and screen recordings are archived at gitlab.com/ai-bench-lab/top-undress-ai-app. Anyone with an iPhone 15 Pro or Pixel 8 and 14 days can reproduce our results within ~5% margin.

Ethical Notes

All test images were AI-generated portraits of consenting adult synthetic personas from a licensed dataset. No real photos were uploaded to any tested service. This is the only ethical way to test this category.

Non-consensual deepfakes are illegal under the EU AI Act (2024), US Take It Down Act (2025), UK Online Safety Act (2023), and Australia Online Safety Amendment (2024). Victims can find help at StopNCII.org.

Limitations

Our benchmark measured mobile experience, not output quality. Two testers is a small sample for the UX score. Network conditions were US-centric; European and Asian users may see different latencies. Some apps changed their free tier during the 14-day window; we used the values that were in effect on day 1 of testing.
